After OCEANVS, I wanted to know whether the exhibition could survive outside the room. An immersive exhibition depends on the body inside it, on darkness, on scale, on sound all around. On a gallery wall, none of that exists. The question was what remains when you cut out a piece of the ocean and give it a frame.

The answer was three objects, each with a screen outside the conventional rectangle: a round display, a square turned into a diamond and bar monitors, industrial screens ordered from a manufacturer in China and imported by Galeria Luis Maluf.

*Oceanvs #1.11 | Ouroboros* is a circle. It deals with the cycle of water, the snake that bites its own tail, where beginning, middle and end become a single matter. The ocean does this all the time: tides that come and go, fauna and flora that renew themselves, birth and death in the same movement.

*Oceanvs #2.22 | Biolumina* is two offset bars. In the depths, where darkness is total, light only appears tied to life. The piece portrays that structure of light and the connections between the points that glow.

*Oceanvs #3.33 | 23º31'54"S 46º30'30"W* is a diamond, and the title is the coordinates of São Paulo. It shows the city submerged, a world in which global warming was not solved and water covered everything, with the bioluminescence of the beings still alive inside it.

The three works were presented by Galeria Luis Maluf at SP-Arte Rotas Brasileiras, at ARCA, in 2023, and remain in the gallery's collection. Each has an edition of five plus one artist's proof.
