A machine reads real news about rare earths. The territory being mined, the power that processes it, the market that prices it. It also reads what gets written about data centres, water and energy. From that material it writes chapters dated 2047, in the voice of Herética Ablation, a synthetic narrator Giselle and I created. The text prints on the spot, on a dot matrix fed by continuous paper, at the slow and noisy pace those machines have. Anyone in the room hears the work writing before they can read it.

The exhibition runs from 15 October to 16 November 2026, at Casa Firjan, in Rio de Janeiro. The duration is part of the work. Over two days, a printer printing is a gesture. Over a month, the paper becomes a material archive, a stack that grows in public and that anyone can measure with their own body. Come back in the third week and there is more paper than you left.

On the wall, each chapter becomes two five-second scenes, generated from its own text. They are made by MiniMax H3, an open video model, running on the work's own machine, next to the printer. The model also generates sound along with the image. The work discards that sound, and the wall stays silent.

H3 does not fit on a consumer graphics card. Together, the models it uses add up to more than 130 GB, and the work's card, an RTX 3090, has 24 GB. To make H3 run there, I took the model apart and rebuilt it piece by piece. The quantization is mine. The road to it began in April 2026 with TurboQuant, my research on compressing language models. The acceleration LoRA is by Larryvrh and brings generation down to six steps. Each 5.17-second scene takes about twelve minutes, with around 54 Wh measured on the card itself. On the studio's RTX 4090, the same setup takes a little over five minutes.

There is no editing and no frame-by-frame selection. The machine generates, the wall shows and the site keeps. In the first batch, the model took the word clay literally, when in 3D it only means an untextured surface, and the digital world came out clay-coloured. Twenty-one scenes were remade with another layer. The rest remain as they came out.

The site heretica.xyz is the other half of the work. Each chapter sits there with its source news in the header and its energy and water consumption in the footer. The account separates what is measured from what is estimated. The writing uses a cloud model, and the water that data centre spends enters the account as an estimate. A piece that speaks about mining, energy and water feeding data centres could not hide its own cost.

I make 2047 with Giselle Beiguelman. She sat on the committee for my final project at FAU-USP in 2014, and now she signs the work with me. The voice of the text comes out of her work. The sources that feed the machine were chosen by the two of us. I built the machine, the writing engine, the H3 quantization and the video system.

Making the invisible visible is what runs through everything I do, from the buried Córrego das Almas in 2012 to the mycorrhizal networks of Floresta Utópica in 2025. Here the invisible is the chain that sustains the writing machine itself. The ore, the water, the energy, the contract. 2047 prints that on paper and lets the stack grow until it takes up volume in the room.
